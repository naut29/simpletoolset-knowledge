# Biological Age Calculator - Longevity Predictor Without Blood Test

## Overview

Estimate biological age without a blood test using four accessible fitness and body-composition markers.

This no-blood-test biological age calculator estimates whether your functional fitness markers look younger, aligned, or older than your chronological age. It combines resting heart rate, maximum push-ups, one-leg balance time, and waist circumference into a transparent fitness-marker model with a what-if simulator.

This tool is available in multiple languages on SimpleToolset (EN, VI, ZH, KO, JA, TH, DE, PT-BR, ES-419, ID, HI, BN, TA, TE, MR).

## Key features

- Bio age test without blood biomarkers
- Uses resting heart rate, strength, balance, and waist markers
- What-if simulator for immediate longevity scenario feedback
- Chart.js comparison of biological age, chronological age, and baseline
- Simple-statistics powered z-scores and percentile estimates
- Private client-side calculation with no account required

## How to use

1. Enter your chronological age and biological sex for reference ranges
2. Add resting heart rate, maximum push-ups, one-leg balance time, and waist circumference
3. Review estimated biological age, age difference, longevity readiness score, and marker percentiles
4. Use the what-if control to adjust one marker, such as reducing waist circumference by 5 cm
5. Compare the chart against chronological age and the population baseline

## FAQs

- Is this the same as PhenoAge?: No. PhenoAge is a clinical biomarker model that uses blood markers. This tool is a no-blood-test fitness-marker estimate inspired by the idea of phenotypic aging, but it is not a clinical PhenoAge calculation.
- Can a biological age calculator diagnose health risk?: No. The result is informational and should not be used for diagnosis, medical decisions, or exercise clearance.
- Why use push-ups and balance?: They are simple functional markers for strength and neuromuscular control, which can reflect broad fitness and aging-related capacity.
- Why does waist circumference matter?: Waist circumference is a practical marker of central adiposity and cardiometabolic risk context, especially when interpreted with other health markers.
- How should I use the what-if scenario?: Change one marker at a time to see how the estimate moves. Treat the change as a planning signal, not a guarantee.
