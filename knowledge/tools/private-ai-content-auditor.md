# Private AI Content Auditor - Local SEO and EEAT Draft Review in Your Browser

## Overview

Audit SEO structure, EEAT-style signals, keyword placement, and local similarity risk for article drafts inside your browser, with rewrite suggestions and a humanizer pass that stay private.

Reviews article drafts in the browser for structural SEO quality, EEAT-style signals, focus-keyword coverage, readability, repeated phrasing, and local similarity risk, then drafts rewrite suggestions and a lighter humanized version without app-server upload.

This tool is available in multiple languages on SimpleToolSet (EN, VI, ZH, KO, JA, TH, DE, PT-BR, ES-419, ID).

## Key features

- Private browser-side review for SEO structure and EEAT-style signals
- Local focus-keyword coverage and density checks
- Local similarity screening against your own pasted comparison text
- Rewrite suggestions plus a humanizer-style tone pass
- IndexedDB session storage for reopening the last audit locally

## How to use

1. Paste an article draft into the browser
2. Optionally add a focus keyword and local comparison text
3. Choose a browser-side inference backend
4. Run the local audit to score structure, originality risk, and keyword usage
5. Review issues, rewrites, similarity flags, and the humanized draft before publishing

## FAQs

- Does it compare my article to the public web?: No. Similarity checks only use the local comparison text that you paste into this route. It does not crawl or search the public web.
- Is the EEAT score an official Google metric?: No. It is a private browser-side heuristic that looks for signals such as structure, evidence, caveats, and experience-style wording. It is not an official Google score.
- What does the humanizer do?: It rewrites parts of the draft locally to reduce stiff or repetitive phrasing. You should still review the result for tone and factual accuracy.
- Does the article leave the browser?: No. The article draft, comparison text, audit report, and humanized output stay inside the browser. Model files may download on first use, but the article itself is not uploaded to the app server.
